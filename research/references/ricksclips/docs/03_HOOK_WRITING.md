# Hook Writing: the Triple Hook

Source: a hook-writing video (full transcript in `reference/triple_hook_transcript.txt`).
Every opening (first 1–2 sentences, ~3 s) must pass all three steps **and** be shown on screen at the same time.

## 1. Context, immediately
Viewers only watch what they have a stake in. The first line must say what the video is about: in the audio, the visuals
and the caption, ideally all three. Delaying the context even to the back half of the first sentence raises the skip rate dramatically.

## 2. The lean
Make the viewer curious in a direction so they lean in instead of opting out. Use proof or a stat, a pain point or benefit,
or something shocking or unexpected.
Test: read the first 1–2 sentences alone. Is the context clear? Is there something that makes you want more?

## 3. The contrarian snapback
Hook them going one way, then yank the line in another direction. Say something that pulls the viewer *away* from where they
were leaning. This is what keeps the curiosity loop open.

## Examples
- *"A city paid a man to make it rain… [context + lean] then refused to pay him when it did." [snapback]*
- *"This man borrowed $175 million… against tanks full of seawater. [context + shock] And the inspectors signed off on every one." [snapback]*

## Visual rule (from all 8 Ricks hooks studied; a slow first frame is where most swipe-aways happen)
Frame 0 is already **mid-action, showing the hook sentence literally**, the camera is already moving fast, and by **2.5 s there's a
second visual beat** (a reveal, impact, glow or follow-the-object). Ricks examples:
- the cannon fires, the camera follows the ball across the sea, and it smashes the hull (within 2.5 s)
- a guard rolls pink paint up the wall on "painted"
- the scratch card glows gold on "winner"
- a scientist shoves a man off a tower and the camera falls with him
- the ticket close-up on "had won a million" while the cashier's face goes shocked
Never open on an establishing shot, an empty set or a slow push.
